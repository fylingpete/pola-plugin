# Two example questions

They show the user what their brain can do, with their own content. Two rounds, always in this order. Both are choice dialogs with two or three ready-made questions; the user picks one and you answer it through the query workflow of the pola-brain skill. The wording in quote blocks is fixed (see "How you talk to the user" in the onboarding skill); `[square brackets]` are filled from the brain.

Never show the user what to "watch for", never explain the mechanics (sources stored first, links, index). The answer itself is the demonstration.

## Round 1: how your brain connects things

Build two or three questions from what is really there. `POLA view --brain R --data` lists every page with its links and the number of its sources; use it instead of reading pages at random.

- **What connects two things.** Two pages that share a neighbour or a source but do not link to each other directly. They need not be people: a person and a project, a company and an idea.
- **Who could help.** A project or idea page, asked against the people pages.
- **Everything about one thing.** The page with the most different sources.

> **Try your brain.** These are example questions that show what your brain can do — pick one:
>
> 1 · What connects [A] and [B]?
> 2 · Who around me could help with [project]?
> 3 · What do I know about [company or topic]?

Start the answer with this line, then answer from the pages and sources, each statement with its source:

> This is an example question, to show you how your brain connects things.

Skip a question the material cannot carry. With a brain that holds only the user's own page, offer the one question it can answer and go on.

## Round 2: where your brain still has gaps

It follows by itself after the first answer; do not ask whether the user wants it.

- Something mentioned on at least two pages that has no page of its own (in the view: a pale dot).
- A person or company page that says very little.
- An obvious question the sources do not answer. Check first that the brain really cannot answer it.

> **And now the other way round: where does your brain still have gaps?** These are example questions too:
>
> 1 · What do I know about [mentioned often, no page of its own]?
> 2 · What am I still missing about [person with a thin page]?

The answer says what is missing and which kind of source would supply it. It invents nothing. End it with:

> Gaps like these are what we fill next.

Then go on to the next step of the onboarding.
